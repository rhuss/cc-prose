Let me be direct: AI code generators are autocomplete on steroids. They're useful. They are not the revolution people keep selling you.

I've used Copilot and Claude for months now. They're great at boilerplate, config files, the kind of repetitive code that makes you want to quit programming. But the moment you need judgment, they fall apart. I've watched a model confidently generate a SQL migration that would have dropped a production column. It compiled. It passed lint. It was wrong.

Here's what I think. The productivity gains are real but narrow. A 2024 Google study found developers completed simple functions faster with AI assistance. Debugging? Architecture decisions? No measurable improvement. That tracks with my experience.

The quality question isn't hypothetical. I've reviewed pull requests where every function was AI-generated and technically correct, yet the overall design made no sense. The code worked in isolation and failed as a system. No tool can save you from not understanding what you're building.

So use the tools. I do. But stop pretending they replace thinking. They don't.
